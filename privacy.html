Privacy notice

Last updated 3 November 2026. This notice follows South Africa’s Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA).

In short

SDA Sabbath runs in your browser. It has no adverts and no tracking cookies, and its maker does not receive your plans, settings, pictures or what you show on screen. Signing in is optional. Once you sign in, usage statistics are sent (they can be switched off at any time, see below). Almost everything stays on the device you use. A few features send a request to another company, only when you press the button for them. They are listed below.

SDA Sabbath is free software made by Lee McQuire; its code is released under the MIT licence. It is published at ssdesk.online. For the optional SDA Sabbath cloud (accounts, usage statistics, stories and messages), Lee McQuire is the responsible party under POPIA.

The church or person who installs and runs SDA Sabbath is responsible for any personal information they type into it (for example names in a service plan or on a banner).

What stays on your device

Your settings, service plans, banners and pictures, imported Bibles and hymnbooks, hymn and video lists, the church details you enter (name, town, service time, giving link) and, if you choose to keep it, your AI key are stored in this browser’s own storage (IndexedDB and localStorage). They are not uploaded. You can erase everything by clearing this site’s data in your browser, or with “Download a fresh copy” and “Reset” in Settings. Backups you export are files you keep yourself; they never include your AI key.

The SDA Sabbath cloud

Accounts, usage statistics and messages are kept in a database run for SDA Sabbath by Supabase, outside South Africa. Only the maintainer can see the statistics and messages; a signed-in person can see their own messages and the replies. Statistics are used only to understand how many churches use SDA Sabbath, how much, and which parts, so it can be improved.

Usage statistics are recorded only while you are signed in. You can switch them off at any time in the first-start setup or in Settings → Account, and nothing more is recorded or sent after that. A story you send is quoted only if you ticked that it may be. Settings → Account → Delete my account deletes your account, your messages and the statistics of the devices you signed in on. You can also ask the maintainer on GitHub to delete the statistics of a device.

Why, and your consent

Most of these are used only to give you the feature you asked for, and only when you use it; using the feature is your consent to that request. The exception is the usage statistics, recorded while you are signed in: you are told what they contain in the first-start setup and in Settings → Account, and you can switch them off there. You can also stop everything by removing your AI key in Settings, or by going offline.

Please do not type personal details into AI buttons

Names, phone numbers, addresses and health or other private details should not be typed into a request that goes to an AI provider, and should be kept out of banners and plans that are shown to or shared with others. Photographs of people need their permission under POPIA.

Your rights

Most information is on your own device: you can see, change and delete it yourself at any time. A message or song you sent can be deleted by the maintainer if you ask (a published shared song is removed from Shared Hymns). To see, correct or delete what the SDA Sabbath cloud holds about you, write to the Information Officer (see “Who is responsible”) or use Delete your account. You may also ask the church that runs SDA Sabbath what it keeps about you. If you believe your personal information has been handled wrongly, you may complain to the Information Regulator (South Africa): inforegulator.org.za.

Children

SDA Sabbath is a tool for church media teams. It does not knowingly collect information from children.

Security

An AI key kept in the browser can be read by anyone who can use that browser profile, and by other pages on the same website address. Use a key with a low spending limit, choose “Forget the key when this window closes” on a shared computer, and remove the key after the service.
